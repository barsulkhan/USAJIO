How the mortgage refinance estimate works

For each loan, the calculator estimates a fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment using the remaining principal, annual interest rate and number of monthly payments. A zero-interest loan is divided evenly across its term. Monthly interest in the annual schedule is calculated from the opening balance and rounded to cents.

Monthly payment change is the current payment minus the new payment. A positive amount means the new payment is lower. Break-even time is estimated closing costs divided by positive monthly payment savings. It is a simple cash-flow estimate and does not include taxes, the time value of money or changes in property costs.

Estimated net interest savings compares the interest scheduled on each loan over its full remaining term, then subtracts the upfront closing costs. A positive result means the new scenario has lower estimated interest after costs; a negative result means it costs more under these assumptions. The comparison assumes you keep each loan for its full modeled term.

What to check before refinancing

  • Compare the same loan type and similar term across lenders, not just the advertised rate.
  • Review lender fees, discount points, lender credits, prepayment penalties and the total cash due at closing.
  • Check whether a lower payment comes from a lower rate or from extending the repayment term.
  • Consider how long you expect to keep the home and whether you will stay past the estimated break-even period.
  • Use the official Loan Estimate and ask the lender to explain any costs that are unclear.

Frequently asked questions

What is the break-even point when refinancing?

It is the approximate time required for monthly payment savings to equal the upfront closing costs. If the new payment is not lower, this simple payment-based method has no break-even point. It does not replace a full comparison of total loan costs.

Can a lower monthly payment cost more overall?

Yes. Extending the repayment term can lower the monthly payment while increasing the total interest paid. Compare the interest totals and the remaining term, not only the monthly amount.

Does this include property taxes and homeowners insurance?

No. This calculator compares principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA charges and escrow amounts may change independently and are excluded.

Does USAJIO show live refinance rates?

No. Enter the rate from a lender's current Loan Estimate. USAJIO does not connect to lenders, request credit information or submit an application.
