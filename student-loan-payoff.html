How this estimate works

Each month, interest is estimated as balance multiplied by APR divided by 12, then the fixed payment is applied. The final payment is reduced to the remaining amount owed.

This is not an official federal student-loan repayment estimator. It excludes income-driven plans, forgiveness, subsidies, capitalization, daily interest, fees, rate changes and deferment/forbearance. Federal and private loan rules differ.

How student loan payoff estimates work

This calculator models one balance with a constant annual rate and a fixed monthly payment. It estimates one month's interest from the opening balance and the annual rate divided by 12, applies the payment, and repeats until the balance reaches zero. If the payment does not cover the estimated monthly interest, the balance will not pay down under this simplified model.

Federal and private student loans can work differently

Federal loans may offer repayment plans based on income, deferment, forbearance, interest subsidies or forgiveness rules. Private student loans have lender-specific contracts and may have different repayment and hardship options. This calculator does not model income-driven payments, forgiveness, daily interest, capitalization, variable rates, fees or multiple loans with different terms. It should not be used to compare or select federal repayment plans.

What to check on your loan statement

  • Current principal balance and the interest rate that applies to each loan
  • Required payment, due date and whether the rate is fixed or variable
  • Whether unpaid interest can capitalize and how extra payments are allocated
  • Any official repayment-plan, deferment, forbearance or forgiveness eligibility

For federal loan options, use the official Federal Student Aid repayment-plan information. For help understanding private and federal loan issues, see the CFPB student-loan resources.
