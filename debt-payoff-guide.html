A Practical Guide to Paying Down Debt

A debt payoff plan should be realistic, sustainable and clear about interest and fees. Start by understanding every balance and the consequences of missing a required payment.

1. Make a complete debt list

Record each balance, annual percentage rate (APR), minimum payment, due date and whether the rate is fixed, variable or promotional. Check the latest statement and lender terms; introductory rates and deferred-interest offers can have important deadlines.

2. Protect required payments

Keep required minimum payments current where possible. Missing payments can lead to fees, penalty terms, credit consequences or collection activity. If a payment is at risk, contact the lender or a reputable nonprofit credit counselor early.

3. Choose a repayment method

Avalanche: direct extra money toward the highest APR debt first while maintaining minimums elsewhere. This often reduces interest cost when rates and fees are comparable.

Snowball: direct extra money toward the smallest balance first. Quick account wins can help motivation, though the total interest may be higher.

Compare both approaches against your cash flow, account terms and ability to stay consistent.

4. Avoid adding new high-cost debt

Where practical, pause new borrowing while repaying balances. Keep a small cash buffer so that an unexpected expense does not immediately return to a credit card.

5. Check the math and the terms

A payment that barely covers monthly interest may reduce principal very slowly; a payment below accrued interest may not reduce it at all. Actual card interest may accrue daily, and fees or new purchases change the result.
