Understanding US Credit Scores and Reports

Credit scores are calculated from information in credit reports. Different scoring models and lenders may weigh information differently, so there is no single score or universal approval threshold.

What commonly influences a score?

  • Payment history: whether required payments are made on time.
  • Amounts owed and utilization: balances relative to available revolving credit can matter.
  • Length of credit history: the age of accounts and how long credit has been used.
  • Credit mix: experience with different types of credit may be considered.
  • New credit activity: recent applications and newly opened accounts can affect some models.

Review reports for accuracy

Check reports from the nationwide credit reporting companies using the official federally authorized source, AnnualCreditReport.com. Review account ownership, balances, payment status and inquiries. Dispute information you believe is inaccurate with the relevant credit reporting company and furnisher, following their instructions.

Build healthy habits

Pay on time, keep revolving balances manageable, apply for credit thoughtfully and monitor reports for errors or unfamiliar activity. Be cautious of companies promising a guaranteed score increase or asking for upfront fees to remove accurate negative information.

Important distinction

A credit score is not the same as a credit report, and a lender may use a score model or additional underwriting criteria that differ from a consumer-facing score.

For authoritative information, consult the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the Federal Trade Commission. AnnualCreditReport.com.
