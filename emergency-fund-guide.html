How to Build an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected, necessary expenses or a temporary loss of income. The right target depends on your obligations, income stability and access to other resources.

Last reviewed: November 22, 2026

1. Estimate essential monthly expenses

Start with costs you would still need to cover during a difficult month: housing, basic utilities, groceries, essential transport, insurance, health needs and required minimum debt payments. Separate these from discretionary spending.

2. Choose a practical first milestone

A small starter reserve can help with common surprises. From there, build toward a target based on several months of essential costs. People with variable income, dependents, specialized employment or limited backup resources may prefer a larger cushion. There is no single target that fits every household.

3. Keep it accessible and separate

Consider a safe, liquid account that is separate from everyday spending. Compare account protections, withdrawal access, fees and current terms. Avoid putting emergency money into assets that can fluctuate sharply or may take time to sell.

4. Automate manageable contributions

Choose a repeatable amount that fits the budget. Windfalls and occasional savings can accelerate progress, but the plan should not depend on them.

5. Refill after use

Use the reserve for genuine urgent needs, then rebuild it. Review the target after major life or expense changes.
