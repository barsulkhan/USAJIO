What to consider before financing a car

Start with the amount financed

The vehicle price is not always the amount you borrow. A down payment or trade-in can reduce the principal, while taxes, title and registration charges, dealer-installed products, and other fees may increase the amount financed. Check the buyer's order and retail installment contract so the calculator input reflects the amount actually borrowed.

Compare the annual percentage rate and the full term

Compare offers using the annual percentage rate (APR), amount financed, number of payments and total of payments—not just the advertised monthly payment. A longer term can lower each scheduled payment while increasing the time interest accrues. A lower payment is not automatically the less expensive loan.

Understand extra payments

An extra principal payment can shorten a fixed-rate loan and reduce interest when the contract allows it and the lender applies the extra amount to principal. Confirm payment-allocation rules and any prepayment terms with the lender. This estimate assumes extra amounts are paid monthly and applied to the balance.

Budget for the full cost of ownership

A loan payment is only one part of owning a vehicle. Insurance, fuel or charging, maintenance, repairs, parking and registration can affect affordability. Keep those expenses in your budget even though this calculator does not include them.

Auto loan calculator questions

Should I use the vehicle price or amount financed?

Use the amount financed shown in the loan paperwork. It may differ from the advertised vehicle price after the down payment, trade-in, taxes, fees and optional products are included.

Does choosing a longer auto loan term save money?

A longer term may reduce the scheduled monthly payment, but it usually means more payments and can increase total interest. Compare both monthly cash flow and total repayment before choosing.

Will extra payments always reduce my interest?

They can reduce interest on a simple-interest amortizing loan when applied to principal, but lender rules and contract terms matter. Ask how extra amounts are allocated and whether any prepayment charge applies.
