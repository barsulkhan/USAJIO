How this estimate works

Uses fixed-rate monthly amortization. Optional extra payments are added to the scheduled payment and reduce principal faster. Enter the amount financed after down payment, taxes and fees.

Actual offers may include taxes, title, registration, dealer fees, add-ons and lender-specific terms not calculated here.

How to estimate the full cost of a car loan

The amount financed is the starting point for a vehicle-loan payment. Begin with the negotiated out-the-door price, then account for sales tax, title and registration charges, dealer documentation fees and any optional products that are included in the contract. Subtract your down payment, eligible rebates and the trade-in value after any amount still owed on the trade-in. The result should match the amount financed shown on the lender's paperwork.

Why the monthly payment is only one part of the comparison

A longer loan term can lower the scheduled monthly payment while increasing the number of months that interest accrues. Compare the APR, amount financed, total interest, total repayment and any optional extra-payment scenario. A dealer offer with a lower payment is not automatically less expensive if the term is longer or the financed amount is larger.

Costs this calculator does not include

  • Fuel, maintenance, repairs, insurance and vehicle depreciation
  • Sales tax, registration, title, dealer fees and optional add-ons unless included in the amount financed you enter
  • Variable rates, balloon payments, lender-specific daily-interest methods or prepayment penalties

Use the amount financed and APR from a written offer when possible. For more context, read the CFPB auto-loan resources and the FTC guide to financing or leasing a car.
