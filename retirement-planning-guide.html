Retirement Planning: Build a Long-Term Savings Plan

Learn how time, contributions, compounding, fees, inflation and investment risk shape a retirement savings plan.

1. Start with a practical picture

List current retirement accounts, regular contributions, employer contributions where applicable, expected retirement timing and spending needs. Keep account balances private; never enter credentials into a planning tool.

2. Understand time and compounding

Investment gains may themselves generate gains over time. The result depends on contributions, fees, taxes, inflation and market performance. Returns are not smooth or guaranteed, and investments can lose value.

3. Review workplace and personal options

Where available, learn how workplace plans, employer matching, vesting schedules, contribution limits and account tax treatment work. These details differ by country and plan. Read official plan documents rather than assuming a calculator's match field represents your actual benefit.

4. Use scenarios, not predictions

Try cautious, middle and optimistic return assumptions. Compare results before and after fees and inflation. Check whether contributions are affordable and whether you have accessible savings for near-term needs.

5. Revisit the plan

Review contributions, beneficiaries, investment mix, fees and goals when circumstances change. Near retirement, consider withdrawals, taxes, healthcare and longevity.
